# Refunds, moving payments, corrections, statements and receipts (ALV-007-C02)

The companion to STORY-007 (`docs/BILLING.md`) and ALV-007-C01 (`docs/BILLING_LEDGER.md`), whose routes and behaviour are unchanged except where this page says so. It adds the money flows the ledger was missing (hand money back, move what a payment pays, correct a payment or adjustment) and the documents a practice gives people (a statement of the account, a receipt for a payment), with a timeline and an aging view. Nothing recorded is ever edited or deleted: every one of these is a new, attributed, audited row.

## What it is, in one paragraph

A **refund** is a new ledger entry that adds to the balance owed and names the payment it hands back; the money taken off that payment's applications is recorded as append-only **release** rows. **Moving** what a payment pays releases part of an application and records a new one for the same amount in one transaction. **Correcting** a payment or adjustment reverses it exactly and records a linked replacement in one save. A **statement** or **receipt** is the ledger read at four cut-offs and one instant; issuing one stores only the cut-offs, a SHA-256 of its canonical content, a number, the template version and a non-financial display envelope (the patient, the addressee, the practice's name, phone and address as displayed, and the currency), never an amount or a balance. The same builder produces the preview, the check at issue, the reprint and the CSV, so they cannot disagree.

Trigger numbers 51157 to 51171 belong to this story; **51172 and up are free**. Rolling the migration back restores both replaced triggers verbatim and removes the new tables and columns; it is only possible while no refund exists, because afterwards the ledger holds money returned and is deliberately not rewritten by a script (the same stance as the earlier ledger migrations).

## Rules

- **Refund.** More than zero and no more than the payment less its earlier refunds; a reversed payment cannot be refunded and a refunded payment cannot be reversed; a reason and one of the active payment methods (how it is returned) are required. The money goes back to **whoever the payment records as having paid it** (the payer, or the account's patient): there is no recipient field, so it cannot go to anyone else. It is taken **first from what the payment has not applied to any charge, then from its applications, newest first**; each application taken from gets a release row for the amount taken, so the charge it paid is open again. A refund never creates credit (the balance only goes up). Entry, releases, the account version and the audit entry are saved together or not at all.
- **Moving.** `releases` name the charges to take money off and `allocations` the open charges to put it on, each named once, positive, in dollars and cents, and the two totals must be exactly equal. Rows are put in charge order before they are compared, so the same request in any order is the same request. A charge cannot be on both sides; nothing can be taken off a charge for more than this payment applied to it; nothing can be put on a charge for more than it has left once the releases are counted; a reversed payment cannot be moved. The releases are saved first and the new applications second **inside one transaction** (the database counts the releases when it checks the applications), so either both happen or neither does, audit entry included. The balance and the payment's unapplied amount do not change.
- **Correcting.** Only a payment or an adjustment: the entry is reversed exactly and a replacement that names it is recorded, in one save. A replacement payment starts **unapplied** (apply it afterwards). A charge cannot be corrected (it can only be reversed); a reversal or a refund cannot be corrected; a refunded payment cannot be corrected; the correction cannot leave the account owing less than nothing. The replacement's save key is the request's key followed by `~2`, so a request key may be at most 62 characters.
- **Same key, same result.** A repeat of a refund, move, correction or issue with the same key and the same request changes nothing and returns the recorded result (even from an out-of-date copy of the account); the same key for a different request is `idempotency_key_reused` (409). A change from an out-of-date copy of the account is the shared 409 concurrency conflict and records nothing.
- **Documents are derived, never a second source of truth.** A preview reads the cut-offs and the content in one repeatable-read transaction. The content depends only on what is at or before the cut-offs, so appending to the ledger never changes it; amounts are fixed two-place text and times fixed UTC text, so the hash never depends on how a number was stored. Issuing needs the preview's token (cut-offs, template version, hash); the content is rebuilt at the token's cut-offs and if it is not byte for byte what was reviewed (a name, the responsible party, an amendment or anything else that reaches this document changed) nothing is issued and the answer is `document_preview_stale` (409). A token that cannot be read is `document_preview_invalid` (400). Numbers go 1, 2, 3 per account and type.
- **Reprint.** The content is rebuilt at the stored cut-offs with the stored envelope and checked against the stored hash; a mismatch is `document_content_changed` (409) and nothing is shown. Each successful showing is audited (`FinancialDocumentReprinted`, who and which document) and each CSV export too (`FinancialDocumentExported`); a refused one is not, because nothing was shown. What has happened since (newer entries, a payment later reversed or refunded) is returned apart as `currentStatus` and never changes the issued content or its hash. The envelope keeps the patient, the addressee and the practice **as resolved on the day** (the party responsible at the document's instant: the latest recorded change at or before the cut-off whose date had arrived, otherwise the registered guarantor, otherwise the patient; a receipt is for the payer), so a reprint shows the same names whatever changed later.
- **Aging.** Each open charge ages in whole UTC days from the performed time of the completed procedure it bills (the charge's recording time when there is none) into 0-30, 31-60, 61-90 and over 90. **Live** aging uses the latest amendment of a performed time; a **document** ages as it did at its own instant (an amendment made after it is ignored), so its reprint still matches its hash.
- **Scope.** One statement per account, a cut-off rather than a period, no consolidated guarantor statement, no PDF library: the browser prints the document (print styles show only the document) and a statement exports as CSV (text a spreadsheet could read as a formula is prefixed with an apostrophe). Re-billing a corrected charge is out of scope.

## Failure-first answers

1. **What if a save fails?** Nothing is stored (one transaction; for a move, both saves) and the answer is `save_failed` (503) without internals; the audit entry is part of the same save, so a refund, move, correction or issue cannot exist without its log entry.
2. **Retries?** The caller retries with the same key (kept on the screen while the request is unchanged); a repeat returns the recorded result. There is no automatic retry and no unbounded loop.
3. **Recovery if retries are exhausted?** Nothing was recorded, so the account is exactly as it was; reload and try again. A document that cannot be rebuilt to match its hash is not shown (`document_content_changed`) and is a defect to investigate, not something to override.
4. **Handled and not handled.** Handled: over-refund, refund of a reversed payment, reversal of a refunded payment, over-release, unequal or no-op moves, two requests racing on one account version, the same document number taken twice, a name or responsibility changing between preview and issue, a log that cannot be written. **Not enforced by the database:** the order in which a refund takes money (unapplied first, then newest applications) and the identity "a payment's net applications are at most the payment less its refunds", because SQL Server's statement-level triggers cannot see a refund and its release rows at the same moment; the service enforces both, `BillingLedgerMath.Verify` detects a breach (`GET .../billing/verification`), and the DB's caps (no over-refund, no over-release, net caps on later applications) bound the damage of a bad writer.
