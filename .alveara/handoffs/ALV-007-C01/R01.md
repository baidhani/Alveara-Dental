## Scope delivered
All seven stop conditions of the prompt (mapped in `R01-evidence/ACCEPTANCE_EVIDENCE.md`):
- **Authoritative ledger on the ALV-N002 money invariant.** Every amount is the `Money` type in code (decimal, USD, two places, banker's rounding) and `decimal(18,2)` in the database; no `double` or `float` anywhere in the billing code (searched). The balance is computed from the entries every time and is never stored.
- **Payment allocation.** A payment is applied to the charges it names or, when it names none, to the oldest open charges first; it is never applied to a charge for more than that charge has left nor for more than the payment holds; what is not applied stays visible as unapplied and can be applied later.
- **Reversals and voids.** A new `Reversal` entry undoes one charge, payment or adjustment once, exactly opposite and with a required reason. Reversing a payment releases its allocations; a charge that payments are applied to cannot be reversed until they are; a reversal cannot be reversed; a reversal that would leave the account owing less than nothing is refused. Nothing recorded is edited or deleted (database triggers).
- **Guarantor responsibility, apart from patient identity.** Billing records who is responsible for an account (the patient or another active patient) from a date, with who and why, append-only. Until billing records one, the account follows the guarantor registered on the patient (ALV-003-C01) and failing that the patient; recording a responsible party never changes the registered guarantor and changing the registered guarantor never rewrites a recorded responsibility. A guarantor sees every account they answer for with each account's own balance and a total; a payment can be recorded as paid by the responsible party.
- **Payment methods owned by the billing domain.** Four built-ins that are always active, plus named methods that each map to one of the four kinds (the kind is what the ledger reports on); inactivated, never deleted or renamed.
- **Reconstruction and verification.** `BillingLedgerMath` rebuilds every derived number from the preserved entries and allocations and `GET .../billing/verification` reports anything that breaks a ledger rule. The screen shows each entry as recorded and, separately, what it stands for now.
- **Idempotency and correlation.** Every create carries a key; the same key returns what the first made and a different request under it is refused. Entries carry a correlation id.
- **The contract for `ALV-008-C01`.** `IProcedureCharging.ChargeCompletedProcedureAsync` makes exactly one charge per billable completed procedure inside the caller's own transaction (see below).
- **Audit.** Five new event types alongside the three from STORY-007, each with the signed-in user and time in the same save; a failed audit write stores nothing.
- **UI.** The Billing tab gains what each entry stands for, reverse and apply actions, an allocation chooser, a reversal form, who is responsible with its history, a guarantor table and payment-method settings.

## Decisions and deviations from the prompt's wording
- **Entry views carry only what was recorded; what an entry stands for now is separate (`standing`).** The first design put "paid / open / reversed" on the entry. A STORY-007 test asserts an earlier entry is identical after a later payment and failed, correctly: derived numbers change as history grows and recorded entries must not. This is also the clearest reading of "payments and adjustments cannot silently rewrite original financial history".
- **A charge with payments applied to it must have them reversed first** (refused with `charge_has_payments`), rather than silently re-allocating them.
- **The four payment kinds stay fixed; billing's methods are named aliases of a kind.** This keeps the STORY-007 database constraint and its tests untouched while meeting "common recorded payment-method configuration owned by the billing domain".
- **Responsibility defaults to the registered guarantor without writing a row**, so STORY-007 accounts, counts and audit entries are unchanged until billing records one. The form to record it is offered once the account exists (like the payment and adjustment forms): the first sweep of the STORY-007 walkthrough failed because the form was offered before anything was billed, which its assertion "no form yet" forbids; the form now appears with the account and the walkthrough passes unchanged.
- **The contract refuses to run outside a transaction**, flushes what the caller staged in it, and derives the charge's save key from the plan procedure.
- No deviation from the prompt's seven stop conditions; no scope was taken from `ALV-007-C02` (statements, receipts, refunds, operational hardening).

## Defects found and fixed by this attempt's own verification
- The STORY-007 test on "history preserved" exposed that derived numbers must not live on the recorded entry (above).
- The database tests found that trigger order is not guaranteed: three scenarios were rearranged so only the intended rule can fire.
- The real browser found a dark-theme colour-contrast failure in the muted text showing what an entry stands for (and on reversed rows), invisible to the unit tests; the custom colour was removed.
- The responsible-party dropdown could show "The patient" while someone else was responsible when that person was neither the patient nor the registered guarantor; the current party is now always an option, with a unit test.
- A first draft of the race test for two simultaneous reversals assumed one particular loser; the loser may instead read the ledger after the winner committed and be told the entry is already reversed. Both outcomes stop a double reversal and the test accepts both.

## Limitations / debt
- **A reversed charge keeps its procedure "billed".** The one-charge-per-procedure rule is a unique index that cannot ignore reversed rows, so a procedure whose charge was reversed is not offered for billing again and cannot be re-billed. Re-billing needs a later story.
- The responsibility form offers the patient, the registered guarantor and the current party; naming any other patient means setting the guarantor in registration first (there is no patient picker in billing).
- A payment is applied to one account; a guarantor paying for several people records one payment per account.
- A reversal needs the account version, so someone working from an out-of-date screen reloads first.
- No refunds, credit balances, statements or receipts (`ALV-007-C02`); no processors or insurance.
- The screen displays server decimals through a JavaScript number (`toFixed(2)`, display only; it never computes with them).
- Frontend: one full `vitest` run during the attempt had one failure in `PatientWorkspace.test.tsx`, a file that does not render billing; it passed alone and in the next full run and its output was not captured, so its cause is unknown (recorded in `R01-evidence/artifacts/test-runs/frontend-gates.txt`). Every later full run passed.
- Mutation checks cover twelve rules, not the whole feature.
