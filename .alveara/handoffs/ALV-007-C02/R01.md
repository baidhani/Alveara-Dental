## Scope delivered
All six stop conditions of the prompt (mapped in `R01-evidence/ACCEPTANCE_EVIDENCE.md`) with the ten binding decisions implemented as written:
- **Refund (D1, D2).** A new `Refund` ledger-entry kind that names its payment, plus append-only `BillingAllocationReleases` rows (the historical negative of part of an allocation). A refund is at most the payment less earlier refunds; a reversed payment cannot be refunded and a refunded payment cannot be reversed; a reason and an active return method are required; the money comes first from what the payment has not applied to any charge and then from its applications, newest first; the recipient is whoever the payment records as having paid it (there is no recipient field), and a refund never creates credit. Entry, releases, account version and audit entry are saved together.
- **Moving what a payment pays (D3).** `releases` and `allocations` rows, each charge named once, positive, whole cents, normalised into charge order before they are compared, equal totals, no charge on both sides, no more taken off a charge than the payment applied to it, no more put on a charge than it has left once the releases are counted. The releases and the new applications are saved in one transaction (two saves, because the database counts releases when it checks applications).
- **Correction and replacement (D4).** A payment or an adjustment is reversed exactly and a replacement that names it is recorded in one save; a replacement payment starts unapplied; a charge cannot be corrected; the account may not be left owing less than nothing before or after the replacement.
- **Documents (D5, D6, D7, D9).** Statements and receipts are derived from the ledger at four cut-offs (last entry, application, release and responsibility change) and one instant by a pure builder, so the preview, the check at issue, the reprint and the CSV cannot disagree. An issued document stores the cut-offs, the instant, a SHA-256 of the canonical content, a per-account number, the template version and a non-financial envelope (the patient, the addressee as resolved on the day, the practice's name, phone and address, and the currency): **no amount and no balance**. A preview carries a token binding the cut-offs, the template version and the hash; issuing rebuilds the content at those cut-offs and answers `document_preview_stale` if it is not what was reviewed. A reprint rebuilds from the stored cut-offs and envelope and answers `document_content_changed` if it does not match the stored hash; what happened since is returned apart as `currentStatus`. Reads are audited (`FinancialDocumentReprinted`, `FinancialDocumentExported`). One statement per account (a cut-off, not a period), HTML print styles and a statement CSV (formula-neutralised), no PDF dependency, no consolidated guarantor statement.
- **Responsibility and aging (D8).** The addressee is resolved from the responsibility history at the document's instant (and kept in the envelope); a receipt is for the payer. Aging counts whole UTC days from the performed time of the completed procedure a charge bills into 0-30, 31-60, 61-90 and over 90; live aging uses the latest amendment, an issued document ages with the amendment effective at its own instant.
- **Timeline.** Every entry, application, release, change of responsible party and issued document in order, each charge naming the completed procedure behind it (with `planItemId` and `completedProcedureId`).
- **Security (D10).** Reads `ViewBilling`; refund, move, correction and issue `ManageBilling` plus CSRF; no new permission; every effect audited in the same save, without PHI in the audit text or the logs; a failed save is 503 `save_failed` and stores nothing.
- **UI.** Refund, move and correct forms that say what will happen before anything is sent, name gaps first and keep what was typed; statement and receipt preview, issue, reprint, print and CSV; the timeline (with a link to the clinical history); aging.

## Decisions and deviations from the prompt's wording
- **Approved decisions implemented as written** (D1-D10). Implementation choices inside them: the replacement entry's save key is the request's key followed by `~2` (so a correction's key may be at most 62 characters); a move is two saves in one transaction; the document instant is the later of the clock and the latest recorded entry; the preview token is the cut-offs, the template version and the hash (it needs no secret, because issuing recomputes the content and a forged token can only be refused as stale).
- **The refund-release ordering is not enforced by the database** (see Limitations): the database enforces every cap (no over-refund, no over-release, net caps on later applications, reversal and refund exclusivity); the order (unapplied first, newest first) and the identity "a payment's net applications are at most the payment less its refunds" are enforced by the service and detected by `BillingLedgerMath.Verify`, because SQL Server's statement-level triggers cannot see a refund and its release rows at the same moment.
- **The move screen offers one source charge and one destination charge**; the API accepts several of each (the rules and tests cover several).
- **The timeline's link to the source goes to the patient's clinical history**, which a billing-only role cannot read; the timeline text still names the procedure. A deep link to one completed procedure was not built.
- **Reprint and export are audited** (decision D10 says "document issue/reprint"); a refused reprint is not, because nothing was shown.
- **One parent test literal was edited** (`BillingSchemaTests`: the example of an invalid kind "Refund" became "Bogus", because Refund is now valid). The assertion, the rule and the test name are unchanged.

## Defects found by this attempt's own verification
- **A real defect in an earlier story's trigger (ALV-007-C01, 51141).** It refused to reverse a charge while any allocation from a live payment existed, even if all of it had been refunded or moved away. The 70-transaction reconciliation hit it on its first run. Fixed in this migration (the trigger counts an allocation net of its releases), with a schema test, a rollback assertion and the walk itself as regression.
- **Two surviving negative controls in the first pass** (N06, the refund reason; N12, the no-credit check after a correction's reversal): each was a missing test; both now killed.
- **Two trigger-order assumptions in my own tests.** 51143 (C01) and 51161 both refuse a refund's wrong method and SQL Server does not say which fires first; the test accepts either and each trigger is now proved alone with the other disabled.
- **An intermittent test of my own** (`Live_aging_follows_a_later_amendment...`): it amended "the first completed procedure", but a completion set finalizes both at one instant, so it picked the wrong one about half the time. It passed in two earlier full runs by luck and failed in the third. It now amends the procedure behind charge 1 explicitly and passed 10 of 10; the new test classes were run three times in a row (99 of 99 each time).
- **A component bug found by its unit test**: the stale-preview message was cleared by the new preview it started.
- **Two dark-theme contrast failures found only by the real browser** (the timeline time stamps; the CSV link).
- **A CSS class clash with the parent walkthroughs** (the new panel reused the status-line class two parent specs locate by) found by the first sweep; fixed in the panel.
- **A real-backend sweep flake**: in the last sweep the odontogram longitudinal walkthrough failed once on a 5-second page-load timeout while the machine was slow (every run in that sweep took about twice as long); two fresh reruns passed 11 of 11. It does not touch billing.
- Several first-run failures were mistakes in the new tests (a miscounted expectation, a selector that matched two elements, a setup field omitted when copying, a number the code was right about); none was a product defect.

## Limitations / debt
- **Database cannot enforce the refund ordering or the net-applications identity** (see above); the service and `Verify` do, and a script that wrote refund and release rows by hand could break them (the caps would still hold).
- **Documents are rebuilt, not stored.** The stored hash and the as-issued reconstruction depend on the builder code and template version staying as they were; changing what a document contains needs a new template version and keeping the old builder (noted for later stories).
- **One statement per account, a cut-off rather than a period; no consolidated guarantor statement; no PDF**; printing is the browser's; an issued document cannot be voided or superseded (a later statement is a new document).
- **The timeline link** goes to the clinical history, which billing-only roles cannot read.
- **Re-billing a corrected or reversed charge** is out of scope (decision D4); the reversed charge keeps its procedure billed.
- **The practice identity is the practice settings row**; if none exists the document has no practice header.
- **A refund by a method that moves real money** (a card processor) is only recorded here; processors are future.
- Mutation checks cover 25 rules, not the whole feature.
